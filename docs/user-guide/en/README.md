# FullFrame user guide

*English · [Español](../es/README.md)*

FullFrame turns a set of photographs into an online exhibition. You can gather
the photographs (your own, or sent by invited authors), have a jury score them
if you want, choose what to show, give the exhibition its look, and open it to
the public.

This guide is for the people who use FullFrame. Each of them sees a different
part of it:

| Who | What they do | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Visitors** | Walk through open exhibitions | [Visiting an exhibition](visitors.md) |
| **Curators** | Prepare, judge, select and publish exhibitions in the **studio** | [Curating an exhibition](curators.md) |
| **Invited authors** | Send their photographs through a personal link | [Sending your photographs](authors.md) |
| **Jurors** | Score the photographs through a personal link | [Being on a jury](jurors.md) |
| **Administrators** | Prepare an exhibition's place in TYDAL, once per exhibition | [Setting up in TYDAL](setting-up-in-tydal.md) |

## FullFrame and TYDAL

FullFrame stores no photographs. They live in **TYDAL**, the asset platform
behind it, inside a **vault**: a protected window onto one exhibition's
photographs. FullFrame keeps the exhibition itself: its texts and look, the
jurors and their scores, the invited authors, and the curator's selection.

That split has some practical consequences, which come up throughout this guide:

- Curators sign in to FullFrame **with their TYDAL account**, and their TYDAL
  role decides what they can do (owners, administrators and editors manage
  exhibitions; viewers can only look).
- Photographs added through FullFrame go straight into TYDAL. Removing one sends
  it to TYDAL's trash, where an administrator can still recover it.
- Deleting an exhibition in FullFrame deletes its jury record and settings, never
  the photographs.

to finish is *Small Wonders*, by the *Atlas Photo Collective*.
